        What Is the Debt Service Coverage Ratio and Why Do Lenders Care?

        The debt service coverage ratio, usually shortened to DSCR or DCR, is a coverage ratio that compares the income a property or business produces with the debt payments due in the same period. A ratio of 1.00 means income matches the payments exactly, anything higher means a cushion, and anything lower means the owner has to find the shortfall elsewhere. Try the free financial ratios calculator to run your own numbers — everything is calculated in your browser and nothing you enter is stored or sent anywhere.

        Commercial lending works differently from a personal mortgage. A lender financing an apartment building or a small shopping centre in commercial real estate is less interested in your salary and more interested in whether the asset itself can repay the debt. That is why creditworthiness checks sit beside a cash flow analysis, and why the ratio is one of the first underwriting numbers anyone quotes. Every landlord who borrows against a building eventually meets the DSCR, because it is the number that tests whether an investment's rent can repay its debt.

        How lenders use the debt coverage ratio

        Underwriters use the ratio as a risk filter. A higher figure signals that rents could fall, or expenses could rise, before the borrower struggles to pay. Most programs publish a minimum, and a deal that clears it moves toward qualification and approval, while one that misses it needs compensating factors such as a bigger down payment, strong reserves or a lower loan amount. Free cash flow after the payment is also a lender's first sign that a borrower can build equity instead of just staying afloat.

        Who should use a DSCR calculator

        • Real estate investors screening a rental property before making an offer
        • Owners of apartments, offices and retail space refinancing a commercial mortgage
        • Small business owners checking whether business income covers a new loan payment and whether they can qualify for expansion capital
        • Brokers comparing several deals against a lender's minimum DSCR

        DSCR Formula: How to Calculate DSCR From Net Operating Income

        The standard DSCR formula is short. You take the property's net operating income (NOI) and divide it by the debt service for the same period, either both annual or both monthly.

        $$\text{DSCR} = \frac{\text{NOI}}{\text{Debt Service}}$$

        Keep the periods consistent. If you enter a monthly NOI, use the monthly payment too; if you use annual figures, use the annual debt service. Mixing the two produces a ratio that is twelve times too large or too small.

        Building your NOI from rent, vacancy and expenses

        NOI is the money a property earns before any loan payments. Start with gross rental income, subtract the income you lose to vacancy, then subtract operating expenses such as maintenance, repairs, property management, insurance and taxes. Loan payments and income tax do not belong in operating expenses.

        \(\text{NOI} = \text{Gross income} - \text{Vacancy loss} - \text{Operating expenses}\)

        Working out annual debt service

        Annual debt service is the sum of the principal and interest payments you make across twelve months. For a fully amortizing loan, the monthly loan payment depends on the balance, the interest rate and the term:

        $$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n}-1}$$

        Here \(P\) is the loan amount, \(r\) is the monthly interest rate (the annual rate divided by 12) and \(n\) is the number of monthly payments. For an interest only period, the payment is simply \(P \times r\), which is why interest only structures show a higher ratio in the early years.

        Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Calculator Example: A Mixed-Use Building Loan

        Here is a complete DSCR calculation walk-through using one set of numbers, so you can follow every step in the DSCR calculator yourself. Suppose you want to refinance a small mixed-use building with a $1,350,000 loan at a 6.85% interest rate, amortized over 25 years.

        Line itemAnnual amount
        Gross rental income$216,500
        Vacancy loss (6%)-$12,990
        Operating expenses-$60,910
        Net operating income$142,600
        Monthly loan payment$9,412.73
        Annual debt service$112,952.75

        Now apply the formula: \(\text{DSCR} = 142{,}600 \div 112{,}952.75 = 1.26\). The building earns 1.26 times its debt service, which means each dollar of loan payment is backed by $1.26 of net operating income and about $29,647 a year is left over. That clears a typical 1.25x minimum, but only barely, a point the next sections return to. Notice that the free cash flow of $29,647 is a pre-tax figure, and a prudent owner also earmarks part of it for repairs and a vacancy rate higher than the 6% assumed here.

        Reading Your DSCR Ratio: What Is a Good DSCR and the Minimum DSCR?

        The number only matters once you compare it with what lenders accept. A good DSCR is one that leaves room for a bad year, and the minimum DSCR is the floor a given program will not go below. Programs differ, and the property type and borrower strength both move the DSCR requirements, so treat the bands below as a guide rather than a promise. A DSCR of 1.0 is the break-even point, where income equals debt service and the owner earns no profit from operations at all.

        DSCRWhat it meansTypical lender view
        Below 1.00Income does not cover the loan paymentsHard to approve without strong compensating factors
        1.00 to 1.24Break even to thin cushionPossible on some investor programs, often with a lower loan-to-value
        1.25 to 1.49Healthy cushionMeets the common commercial minimum
        1.50 and aboveStrong cash flowEasier approval and better pricing

        Our example at 1.26 sits in the healthy band, but a 10% drop in NOI would pull the ratio down to 1.14, so the cushion is thinner than it first looks. Many lenders also check the ratio against the appraisal value, since a property that appraises low can shrink the loan and change the payment.

        DSCR Loans for Rental Property Investors

        A DSCR loan is a business purpose mortgage approved mainly on the property's rent rather than on the borrower's tax returns or pay stubs. These DSCR loans are sold as non-QM products to investors buying or refinancing a single-family home, a small multifamily building, a short-term rental or a portfolio of properties. For many borrowers the DSCR loan calculator is the first screen, because it shows whether a rental property clears the lender's line before any paperwork is signed, and it is the quickest way to learn whether you can qualify.

        The rent divided PITIA version of DSCR

        Residential investor programs usually skip NOI entirely. They divide the market rent by PITIA, which is the monthly principal, interest, taxes, insurance and HOA dues. Because it ignores maintenance and vacancy, this ratio runs higher than the NOI-based figure for the same property.

        \(\text{DSCR} = \dfrac{\text{Monthly rent}}{\text{PITIA}}\)

        • Principal and interest: the monthly loan payment on the balance
        • Taxes and insurance: property taxes and the hazard insurance premium
        • HOA dues: association fees, if the property has any
        • Rent: the lower of the lease rent or the appraiser's market rent

        Common DSCR loan requirements and the DSCR loan term

        Expect a credit score minimum, a down payment of roughly 20% or more, cash reserves and a ratio at or above the program minimum. A lower ratio often caps the loan-to-value (LTV), so a property that barely covers its payments might qualify for 70% LTV where a stronger one gets 80%. Some lenders also offer a cash out refinance option with its own LTV limit. The most common structure is a 30 year fixed rate with full amortization, though a five or ten year interest only period is available on some programs, and the lender may call for an appraisal to confirm market rent.

        Short-term rentals, BRRRR and portfolio financing

        An Airbnb-style short-term rental is underwritten on projected or trailing booking income instead of a lease, so lenders tend to apply a stricter ratio. A BRRRR investor (buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat) runs the calculation twice: once on the stabilized rent to refinance, and again on a blended portfolio loan that bundles several rentals. Each stage has to clear the program's minimum DSCR before the lender releases the equity as capital for the next purchase, and a healthy ratio also protects the owner's cash on cash return and return on investment, since cheaper debt leaves more of the rent in the owner's pocket. Good financing terms depend on the ratio staying above the minimum across every property in the set.

        Screening a Fourplex With the DSCR Loan Calculator

        Marisol is under contract on a four-unit building for $412,500 and wants a 75% loan, so she opens the calculator before her lender call. The tax bill is 1.82% of the price ($625.63 a month) and the insurance quote is $1,860 a year ($155.00 a month). She enters a $309,375 balance, a 7.125% rate and a 30-year term, which gives a principal and interest payment of $2,084.32.

        The four units rent for $3,340 a month combined, based on the appraiser's market rent schedule. Her PITIA comes to $2,864.94, and the debt service coverage ratio the calculator returns is 1.17 ($3,340 divided by $2,864.94).

        That number matters because the lender's published program minimum is 1.20. At 1.17 the deal fails by three hundredths, so Marisol changes one input: she lowers the loan-to-value to 70%, which cuts the balance to $288,750 and the payment to $1,945.36. With taxes and insurance unchanged, PITIA falls to $2,725.99 and the ratio rises to 1.23, comfortably over the 1.20 floor.

        • At 75% LTV: 1.17, below the 1.20 minimum
        • At 70% LTV: 1.23, above the minimum
        • Extra cash needed: $20,625 more down payment ($412,500 × 5%)

        She decides to ask her lender to price the 70% structure, and to call the insurance agent about a higher deductible that would trim the premium. Because the calculator showed exactly how far the ratio sat from the line, she walks into the call with a specific counter-offer instead of a general question.

        Maximum Loan Amount and Interest Rate Sensitivity

        You can run the formula backwards to find the largest loan your income supports: divide NOI by your target DSCR, then convert that debt service back to a balance. For our building, a target of 1.25 supports a maximum loan amount of about $1,363,500, while a more cautious 1.35 target reduces it to about $1,262,500.

        The interest rate has an equally large effect, because it changes the payment on the same balance. The table keeps the $1,350,000 loan and the $142,600 NOI fixed and moves only the rate.

        One extra percentage point takes the building from comfortable to borderline. An interest only period works in the opposite direction: with interest alone the annual payment is $92,475, and the ratio rises to 1.54 until amortization starts.

        Global DSCR and Other Debt Coverage Variations

        Some small business and smaller multifamily lenders go beyond the property and calculate a global DSCR. This version adds the borrower's personal income and personal debts to the property figures. A borrower with high income and little debt may see a global ratio well above the property's own, while someone carrying heavy credit card balances may be declined on a deal that looks fine on paper.

        Other lenders use debt service that includes more than principal and interest, or ask for the ratio on a trailing twelve-month basis. Always confirm how a specific lender defines income and debt obligations before you compare your result with their minimum. Whatever a lender or finance team calls the figure, the arithmetic is the same NOI divided by debt service, so compare how each defines income rather than comparing labels.

        Debt Service Coverage Ratio Calculator in Excel or Google Sheets

        You can reproduce this debt service coverage ratio calculator in an Excel or Google Sheets workbook with three cells. Put NOI in one cell, compute the payment with the PMT function in another, multiply by 12, and divide NOI by the result. Sheets are handy for stress tests, such as lowering rent by 10% or raising the rate by a full point, but a dedicated calculator removes the risk of a broken formula.

        How to Improve Your Coverage Ratio

        If the number falls short, you can move either side of the fraction. Work through these steps in order, running the calculation again after each change:

        1. Raise rents toward market levels where leases allow it.
        2. Reduce vacancy by shortening turnover time between tenants.
        3. Trim operating expenses by rebidding insurance, maintenance and management contracts.
        4. Make a larger down payment so the loan amount, and therefore the payment, shrinks.
        5. Choose a longer amortization or an interest only period to lower the payment.
        6. Shop for a lower interest rate, since even a small reduction improves the ratio.

        Treat the DSCR calculator output as a screening tool: lender programs differ in how they treat fees, reserves and property type, so a calculated ratio is an estimate, not an approval.

        Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR) Calculator questions

        What does DSCR stand for?

        DSCR stands for debt service coverage ratio. It compares the income a property or business produces with the loan payments due in the same period, so a result of 1.00 means income exactly covers the debt.

        How do you calculate the debt service coverage ratio?

        Divide net operating income by debt service for the same period. For a rental property loan, many lenders instead divide the monthly rent by PITIA, which adds taxes, insurance and HOA dues to the principal and interest payment.

        What is a good DSCR?

        Many commercial lenders look for 1.25 or higher, which leaves a cushion if income dips. A result of 1.50 or more is considered strong, while 1.00 is break even and anything lower means income does not cover the loan payments.

        What DSCR do I need to qualify for a DSCR loan?

        Minimums vary by lender and program, commonly between 1.00 and 1.25 for investor rental loans. A lower ratio may still work with compensating factors such as a larger down payment, strong credit or cash reserves.

        What is the difference between NOI and gross income?

        Gross income is all the rent collected. Net operating income subtracts vacancy and operating expenses such as maintenance and repairs, but not the loan payment or income tax.

        What is a global DSCR?

        A global DSCR adds the borrower's personal income and personal debts to the property's income and debt. Some lenders use it for small business and smaller multifamily loans.

        Does an interest only loan change the DSCR?

        Yes. During an interest only period the payment contains no principal, so debt service is lower and the ratio is higher, then it drops once full amortization begins.
