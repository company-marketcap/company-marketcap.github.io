        How the Specialty Vehicle (SV) Loan Calculator Works

        A specialty vehicle loan is financing for a recreational or work vehicle that sits outside a standard sedan purchase: an RV, a travel trailer, a boat, an ATV, a tractor or a motorcycle. The calculator below treats it the way a lender does. It starts from the total purchase price, adds the taxes and fees you owe at delivery, subtracts what you bring to the deal, and amortizes the remainder across your term in months. If you want to see how the figures change, the free biweekly auto loan calculator gives you an instant result you can adjust as you go.

        You can work in two directions. Enter a price and see the payment, or enter the payment you can live with and see how much vehicle that buys. Either way, a loan payment calculator gives an estimate until your final APR and terms are approved.

        The amount you finance

        The loan amount is built from every line of the deal, not only the sticker price. In plain terms it is the price, plus sales tax, plus fees, minus your trade allowance, plus any balance still owed on the trade, minus your cash and rebates.

        $$\text{Loan} = P + T + F_{nt} + F_{t} - A + O - R$$

        Here \(P\) is the purchase price, \(T\) the sales tax, \(F_{nt}\) and \(F_{t}\) the non-taxable and taxable fees, \(A\) the trade allowance, \(O\) the amount owed on trade and \(R\) your rebates and cash down.

        The monthly payment formula

        Once the amount is known, the loan payment comes from the standard amortization formula, where \(r\) is the monthly rate (the annual interest rate divided by 12) and \(n\) is the number of months.

        $$M = L \times \frac{r}{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}$$

        This is the same math behind any auto loan calculator, which is why one tool can handle a pickup, a pontoon boat or a motorhome.

        Inputs to Enter in a Car Loan Calculator for Specialty Vehicles

        Every field changes the result, so it helps to know what each one means. Gather these figures from your dealer's buyer's order before you start. If you want to see how the figures change, the motorcycle rv and boat loan calculator gives you an instant result you can adjust as you go.

        Total purchase price

        This is the cost of the vehicle plus options and destination charges, with no sales tax included. The tool adds tax for you and shows your after-tax price.

        Rebates and cash down

        Cash you pay up front from your savings and any factory rebates reduce what you borrow. A larger cash down payment means a smaller loan and less interest over the life of the note.

        Trade allowance

        The value the dealer gives your old vehicle. In many states, a trade-in also lowers the amount that is taxed.

        Amount owed on trade

        If you still owe money on the vehicle you are trading, that outstanding balance rolls into the new loan, so enter it here.

        Taxable fees and non-taxable fees

        Title transfer fees are usually taxable, while document fees are often not. Keep the two apart so the tax lands on the right base. Mixing up fees is one of the most common reasons an estimate misses the lender's figure.

        Sales tax rate

        Use your local percentage. A few places, including California, Hawaii, Michigan and the District of Columbia, give no sales tax deduction for a trade-in, so check the box for that rule if it applies where you live.

        Interest rate and term

        Enter the annual interest rate from your pre-approval and the loan term in months. Specialty lenders often allow long terms, such as 120 or 144 months, for pricier units.

        Worked Example: Financing a Travel Trailer with the Payment Calculator

        Say you are buying a 28-foot travel trailer. Here are the inputs and what the payment calculator returns.

        InputValue
        Total purchase price$64,850
        Trade allowance$11,200
        Amount owed on trade$4,300
        Rebates and cash down$7,500
        Non-taxable fees$395
        Taxable fees$240
        Sales tax rate6.35%
        Annual interest rate7.89%
        Term144 months

        The taxable base is $64,850 minus the $11,200 trade allowance plus $240 in taxable fees, or $53,890. At 6.35% that makes $3,422.02 in tax. The amount financed is therefore $64,850 + $3,422.02 + $395 + $240 − $11,200 + $4,300 − $7,500 = $54,507.02.

        At a 0.6575% monthly rate over 144 months, your monthly payment is $586.73. Across the whole note you repay $84,489.13, of which $29,982.11 is interest paid to the lender.

        Term changes the total cost

        Run the same loan at different lengths and the trade-off becomes clear. A shorter term costs more each month but far less overall.

        Longer term loans lower your monthly payments but raise the interest. Short term lengths do the opposite, which is why the best choice depends on your budget, not on the lowest number alone.

        Dana has a signed quote on a 22-foot pontoon boat at $38,640 and a take-home pay of $6,850 a month. The credit union's pre-approval says 8.49% APR, so that goes in too, along with a 120-month term.

        The tax comes out at $2,814.45 on a taxable base of $38,820, which makes the amount financed $35,759.45. The result for the payment is $443.17 a month, with $17,421.52 of interest over the full ten years.

        Dana checks that figure against the 10% to 15% rule for a monthly auto loan payment: 10% of take-home pay is $685.00 and 15% is $1,027.50, so $443.17 is well under both. The result clears that guideline easily, but the interest bothers Dana.

        So Dana reruns the calculation with exactly one input changed, the term, now 96 months. The payment rises to $514.47, still about 7.5% of take-home pay, and total interest falls to $13,629.76, saving $3,791.76. Dana asks the loan officer for the 96-month quote and decides to put the $71.30 monthly difference toward insurance and winter storage instead.

        Auto Loan Rates, Credit Score and Down Payment: What Moves Your Payment

        The lowest rates generally go to each borrower with high credit scores, large down payments and short terms. A credit union may offer less than a bank, though that depends on the lender. Your credit score is one of the first things a lender checks, and a stronger score can cut your annual APR noticeably. Those are the figures you type into the rate field of this calculator, and they apply to RV, boat and trailer financing just as they do to a car.

        The down payment matters just as much. In the example above, raising cash down from $7,500 to $12,500 drops the amount financed to $49,507.02 and the monthly payment to $532.91, a saving of $53.82 every month.

        How much you can afford

        Many lenders suggest keeping a monthly auto loan payment to about 10% to 15% of your after-tax take-home pay. Work backward from that figure to decide how much you can afford, then test it against the affordability of insurance, storage and upkeep, which the loan does not cover.

        Credit and approval

        Rates for a specialty vehicle loan vary with credit and collateral. The collateral is the vehicle itself, and age matters: lenders cap the term for older units, and once you are prequalified, a preapproval shows what you can borrow before you shop.

        Specialty Vehicle Loans vs a Standard Auto Loan or Car Loan

        A standard auto loan or car loan covers a daily driver, and lenders price it on a long history of resale values. Specialty vehicle loans cover assets that are used less often and experience a different depreciation curve, so the terms, rates and maximum ages can differ.

        • Boat, RV, motorcycle, ATV, travel trailer and tractor financing
        • Purchase or refinance, new or used loans
        • Extended loan terms for more affordable payments

        Collector car and classic car loans

        A collector car or classic car loan is its own niche. These lenders may allow 180 months and look at vehicle year, down payment and condition when they set your rate. A watercraft or motorcycle follows similar logic. Enter these longer terms, up to 180 months, in the calculator to see how far the payment drops.

        Refinance and payoff

        If rates fall, you can refinance to a new note. Extra principal also speeds up loan payoff and trims what you owe, so check your payoff balance before you decide. Rerun the calculator with the new rate and term to see whether refinancing actually lowers your payment.

        Getting an Exact Payment Quote Instead of an Estimate

        A calculator gives an estimate. Your final payment can shift once the lender fixes your APR, term and fees, so treat the result as a range, not a promise. To tighten it up before you ask for an exact quote:

        • Use the out-the-door number: the purchase price plus taxes and fees
        • Test two cash down amounts to see how the payment moves
        • Compare a couple of terms to balance the payment against the total cost
        • Run two or three scenarios to find a monthly range you are comfortable with

        Once you have a target, apply for a payment quote from a lender that finances your type of vehicle, and ask what the loan balance would be after 12 months.

        Specialty Vehicle (SV) Loan Calculator questions

        What is a specialty vehicle loan?

        A specialty vehicle loan finances a recreational or work vehicle outside a standard car purchase, such as an RV, travel trailer, boat, motorcycle, ATV or tractor. Terms and rates depend on your credit, the vehicle's age and the collateral.

        How is the amount I finance calculated?

        Start with the purchase price, add sales tax and fees, subtract your trade allowance, add any balance still owed on the trade, then subtract your rebates and cash down. The result is the amount you borrow.

        Does a trade-in lower my sales tax?

        In most states the tax is figured on the price less your trade-in. A few places, including California, Hawaii, Michigan and the District of Columbia, tax the full price, so check the no-deduction box if that applies to you.

        Is a longer term better?

        A longer term lowers the monthly payment but increases the total interest you pay. Compare two or three terms to balance the payment against the overall cost.

        How much should I spend on a monthly payment?

        Many lenders suggest keeping a monthly vehicle loan payment to about 10% to 15% of your after-tax take-home pay. Remember insurance, storage and maintenance on top of the loan.

        Is the result my exact payment?

        No. It is an estimate. Your lender's final APR, term, fees and approval can change the figure, so request a quote for your specific vehicle.

        How does my down payment change the loan?

        A larger cash down payment reduces the amount you finance, which lowers both the monthly payment and the interest paid over the life of the loan.
