        How the Clergy Housing Allowance Worksheet Calculates Your Exclusion

        Section 107 of the tax code lets a minister of the gospel leave a housing or rental allowance out of gross income, as long as it is used to provide a home. The IRS explains in Publication 517 that you exclude the smallest of three amounts, so each line of the worksheet feeds one of them: The checkbook balancer is free to use with no sign-up, and works on desktop and mobile.

        1. Amount designated: the sum your employer set aside as a housing allowance by official action, before it was paid.
        2. Amount actually paid: the housing expenses you really incurred during the calendar year.
        3. Fair rental value: what the home would rent for, furnished, plus utilities.

        Your exclusion is the lowest of these three figures, written as:

        $$\text{Excludable allowance} = \min(\text{Designated},\ \text{Actually Spent},\ \text{Fair Rental Value} + \text{Utilities})$$

        The leftover is the excess allowance:

        $$\text{Excess allowance} = \text{Designated} - \text{Excludable allowance}$$

        If the designation is the smallest number, the excess is zero and you have simply left savings on the table. If you spent more than you designated, the extra spending cannot be excluded at all, which is why the designation step matters as much as the arithmetic.

        Housing Expenses You Can List on the Housing Allowance Form

        Almost anything that provides or maintains the home belongs on the worksheet. The housing expenses that qualify include: Pair this with the free benefit of spending less calculator for a fuller picture before you make a decision.

        • Rent, or down payment and principal and mortgage interest payments if you own the home
        • Property taxes, even when they also appear as itemized deductions
        • Utilities such as heat, electricity, water and basic telephone
        • Furniture, appliances, dishes, rugs, curtains and other furnishings
        • Home insurance, HOA fees and a home security system
        • Repairs and upkeep, from a new water heater to lawn care and cleaning supplies

        Groceries, personal clothing, toiletries and maid service never qualify. A spreadsheet works well for tracking the eligible costs month by month, and a single dedicated bank account or card makes year-end totals much easier.

        Why receipts and records matter

        The liability for the amount you exclude is yours, not the church's. Keep original receipts, invoices and canceled checks for every item, because the records are what you would show the IRS if it ever asked you to justify the exclusion.

        Fair Rental Value: The Ceiling on Your Parsonage Allowance

        Congress added the fair rental value language to the code in 2002, so the parsonage allowance you exclude can never exceed what the home, its furnishings and the garage would rent for, plus the cost of utilities. This is sometimes called the fair market rental value of the property. A real estate agent or appraiser can give you a written estimate, and the person should be unrelated to you, so the figure holds up as an arm's-length opinion.

        A homeowner with a paid-down mortgage often finds this ceiling is the lowest of the three numbers. That is not a mistake in the worksheet; it simply means the market value of your housing is lower than what you spent, and the exclusion stops there.

        Parsonage provided by the church

        When the congregation owns the house, you do not report its rental value as income, but you can still designate part of your cash pay to cover furniture, repairs and other costs of the parsonage provided to you. That amount must also be declared in advance.

        Minister's Housing Allowance Worked Example for a Homeowner

        Take a minister whose church pays a salary of $36,500 and designates $34,500 as a housing allowance for the year, for total cash compensation of $71,000. The home is owned, and the annual housing expenses come to:

        Housing expenseAnnual amount
        Mortgage payments (principal and interest)$16,950
        Property taxes$3,186
        Home insurance$1,240
        Utilities$4,368
        Furniture and appliances$2,275
        Repairs and upkeep$1,610
        HOA fees$480
        Total actually spent$30,109

        A local appraiser puts the furnished home, with its garage, at $27,600 a year in rent, so fair rental value plus utilities is $27,600 + $4,368 = $31,968. Now compare the three figures:

        TestAmount
        Amount designated$34,500
        Amount actually paid$30,109
        Fair rental value plus utilities$31,968
        Excludable housing allowance (lowest)$30,109

        The excess allowance is $34,500 − $30,109 = $4,391. That amount is taxable income, reported as wages on line 1h of Form 1040 with the words "Excess allowance" and the amount written on the dotted line beside it. The next year's request could include a small cushion over the $30,109 spent, but not a large one, because unused housing dollars always come back as taxable income.

        Clergy Housing Allowance Form Walkthrough: A Renting Pastor Checks the Numbers

        It is the first week of December, and Pastor Elena Marsh, an associate minister who leases a two-bedroom apartment, has to hand the board a figure by Thursday. Her current resolution designates $26,800, and she wants to know whether that was too high, too low or right before she asks for next year's amount.

        She opens the housing allowance exclusion calculation and enters what she actually paid: twelve lease payments of $1,725 ($20,700), renters insurance of $214, utilities of $3,942, and $1,873 for a sofa, a desk and a mattress bought in March. The total comes to $26,729. The landlord's current listing for a comparable furnished unit is $2,025 a month, so fair rental value is $24,300, and with utilities added it reaches $28,242.

        TestAmount
        Designated$26,800
        Actually paid$26,729
        Fair rental value plus utilities$28,242

        The lowest figure is the $26,729 she spent, so that is her exclusion, and the excess is only $71. Pub 517's rule is satisfied, and she will report the $71 as an excess allowance on line 1h of Form 1040.

        The result tells her two things. First, the designation was nearly perfect, so the decision is to keep the same structure rather than inflate it. Second, the fair rental value ceiling of $28,242 leaves $1,513 of room above her spending, so a request of $27,500 next year, which covers a planned $700 rent increase, stays safely under every limit. She writes that number on the request, attaches the worksheet, and sends it to the board before the meeting.

        Who Qualifies for a Minister's Housing Allowance

        You must be ordained, licensed or commissioned, and you must perform ministerial services: conducting worship, administering sacraments, and carrying management responsibility in the local church or denomination. The home must be paid for as compensation for those duties.

        Bi-vocational ministers

        If you also hold a secular job, the allowance can come only from your ministerial pay. For a part-time or bi-vocational minister, designating up to all of the church's cash compensation can still be reasonable.

        Retiree and retirement income

        A retiree can often keep the benefit. Retirement payments from a church pension plan can be designated as a housing allowance by the denomination or plan, which gives a retired minister the same exclusion for the year's housing costs. Because retirement plans differ, confirm the designation rules with your plan administrator.

        Housing Allowance Designated in Advance by Official Action

        Treasury Regulation 1.107-1 requires that the amount be designated in advance of payment, by official action of the employing church or qualified organization. In practice that means a board resolution, a line in the budget, an employment contract or the minutes of the meeting. Writing it down is the best practice, and the document never has to be attached to your return.

        • You decide the amount and submit your request to the church board by its November or December meeting.
        • The board approves it by resolution before the new year, never retroactively.
        • If you move mid-year or costs change, the board can amend the designation up or down, but a larger figure must be approved before the added money is spent.

        The designation must follow the calendar year, even if the church's fiscal year runs differently. Newly hired ministers often get a safety-net resolution that splits each paycheck, such as 60% salary and 40% housing, until the full-year request is approved.

        Rental Allowance for Clergy Who Lease Their Home

        If you lease your home, the same three-part test applies, and the first line of the form is simply your yearly lease payments. Most clergy who lease find the amount actually used for housing is close to the amount designated, since monthly payments are predictable. Add renters insurance, utilities and any furniture you bought, and compare the total with what the place would rent for furnished. Ministers who sublet a room or share a household should list only their own share of each bill, so the numbers reflect what you personally paid to provide a home.

        Reasonable Compensation Limit on a Rental Allowance

        The tax code sets no dollar or percentage cap on the rental allowance, but the whole package, salary and designation together, has to be reasonable compensation for the services you provide. A board should rarely override a minister's request with a smaller figure, because the church cannot cause you to exclude too much, yet it can cause you to pay more tax than necessary.

        Excess Allowance and Self-Employment Tax on Your Return

        The housing allowance is excluded for federal income tax, but it is not excluded for self-employment tax. Ministers are usually treated as self-employed for Social Security and Medicare, so you add your wages and the housing allowance to net earnings on Schedule SE. For the example above, the roughly $66,609 of salary plus excluded housing is multiplied by 92.35% and then by 15.3%, before other adjustments, which is about $9,412.

        The excess allowance, by contrast, goes straight onto Form 1040 as wages. If your church issues a W-2, the designated allowance is usually shown in Box 14 rather than in the wage boxes, and tax software with a clergy worksheet will pull those lines for you. Because no income tax is withheld on ministerial pay unless you request voluntary withholding, plan on making estimated tax payments each quarter.

        Mistakes That Put Your Housing Exclusion at Risk with the IRS

        Most problems come from timing and paperwork rather than from the math. The IRS has challenged ministers in a few predictable situations, and a quick check of the worksheet helps you avoid each one:

        • Designating the allowance after the money was already paid, which makes that portion ineligible.
        • Claiming costs that never went to the home, such as groceries or personal clothing.
        • Counting the same expense twice, for example listing a mortgage payment and again as a separate down payment.
        • Forgetting that the allowance is still subject to self-employment tax.
        • Letting the designation lapse in January because no new resolution was passed.

        Clergy tax rules reward careful documentation. When an examiner asks how the exclusion was figured, a dated worksheet, the board's approval and a folder of receipts answer the question in minutes. The IRS can only accept the portion actually used to provide or maintain the home, so a thin paper trail turns a legitimate exclusion into an argument.

        Using Your Housing Allowance Worksheet Results for Tax Savings

        The benefit is real: a minister in the 22% bracket who excludes $30,109 avoids roughly $6,624 of income tax, and that tax savings can fund extra retirement contributions or health costs. The employer pays nothing extra, so there is no cost to the congregation.

        Tip: Print the completed worksheet, sign and date it, and file it with your housing allowance request so the board has a record of how the number was reached.

        Run the worksheet twice a year, once in the autumn to size the next request and again in January to compare the actual figures with the designation, so any gap is visible well before filing season.

        Clergy Housing Allowance Worksheet questions

        What is a clergy housing allowance?

        It is part of a minister's pay that the church designates, in advance, as a housing allowance. It can be left out of gross income for federal income tax, up to the lowest of three amounts, but it is still subject to self-employment tax.

        How much of my housing allowance can I exclude?

        You exclude the smallest of the amount your church designated, the housing expenses you actually spent during the year, and the fair rental value of your furnished home plus utilities. Anything above that smallest amount is taxable.

        Does the church have to approve the housing allowance before I spend the money?

        Yes. The amount must be designated in advance of payment by official action of the church or qualified organization, such as a board resolution or meeting minutes, and the designation follows the calendar year.

        What housing expenses count on the worksheet?

        Rent or mortgage and down payments, property taxes, utilities, furniture and appliances, insurance, HOA fees, repairs and upkeep. Groceries, personal clothing, toiletries and maid service do not count.

        Where do I report an excess housing allowance?

        Include the amount you cannot exclude as wages on line 1h of Form 1040 and write 'Excess allowance' and the amount on the dotted line next to it.

        Is the housing allowance subject to self-employment tax?

        Yes. It is excluded for income tax but not for self-employment tax, so it is included in net earnings on Schedule SE for ministers treated as self-employed.

        Can a retired minister claim a housing allowance?

        Often yes. Retirement payments from a church plan can be designated as a housing allowance by the denomination or plan, so confirm the rules with your plan administrator.
