        Percentage Discount Calculator: How to Calculate a Discount

        A percentage discount removes a share of the original price. The discount formula is the same one used for any percentage decrease: you find the discount amount first, then subtract it from the original price to get the sale price.

        $$\text{Discount amount} = \text{Original price} \times \frac{\text{Discount \%}}{100}$$

        $$\text{Final price} = \text{Original price} - \text{Discount amount}$$

        The shortcut is to multiply the original price by the share you still pay. A 30% markdown means you pay 70% of the tag price, so Final price = Original price × 0.70. Both routes give the same discounted price, and the shortcut saves one step when you are shopping on your phone.

        1. Write down the price before discount, which is the original price on the tag.
        2. Convert the discount rate to a decimal by dividing by 100.
        3. Multiply the original price by that decimal to get the amount saved.
        4. Subtract the amount saved from the original price to get the reduced price.

        Finding the Discount Amount and the Discounted Price

        The discount amount is the money that stays in your pocket, while the discounted price is what the register asks for. Keeping the two numbers apart matters because stores often print only one of them, and a clear step-by-step habit prevents mistakes when the price has cents in it. A percent discount calculator does this split for you, and it also records the saved amount as its own line, so your total savings are visible at a glance rather than buried inside the final figure. Writing the formula out once on paper, then letting the tool confirm it, is the quickest way to trust the numbers.

        It also helps to remember the limits of the formula. A discount can never push the final price below zero, and a discount percentage above 100 makes no sense for a normal purchase. If your result looks too good to be true, recheck that you typed the original price, not the sale price, into the first field.

        Every discount calculator, including this one, relies on the same two lines of arithmetic, so a quick mental check is always possible: round the price, take 10% by moving the decimal point, and scale up from there.

        How to Calculate Discount Percentage from Two Prices

        Sometimes the tag shows only a before and an after. To calculate discount percentage, subtract the sale price from the original price, divide the difference by the original price and multiply by 100. The result is your discount percentage, a figure you can use to compare one deal with another no matter how different the prices are.

        $$\text{Discount \%} = \frac{\text{Original price} - \text{Sale price}}{\text{Original price}} \times 100$$

        Percentage Off with Sales Tax

        In most places the tax is charged on the discounted price, not on the original, so the discount also shrinks the tax bill. Here the tax comes to $23.52 instead of the $33.60 you would pay on the full tag. Check your receipt, because local rules for VAT and sales tax differ by product and by region.

        Fixed Amount Discount with a Coupon Code

        A fixed amount works differently: a coupon code that takes $60 off the same mixer leaves $403.50. That is only a 12.94% discount percentage, so the 30% promotion is clearly the better deal. Converting every coupon into a percent off is the fastest way to rank offers.

        Stacked Discounts and the Percent Off Calculator

        When two offers apply one after another, they are multiplicative, not additive. The first discount is taken from the original price, and the second discount is taken from the already reduced price. Take 30% off the mixer, then an additional 12% off that sale price: $324.45 × 0.88 = $285.52. You save $177.98, and the effective discount is 38.4%, not 42%.

        $$\text{Effective discount} = 1 - (1 - d_1)(1 - d_2)$$

        Double Discount Math

        A double discount always lands below the simple sum of the two percentages, because the second cut applies to a smaller base. That is why a chain of small coupons feels weaker than the headline numbers suggest, and why this percent off calculator reports the effective discount next to the final price.

        Working Backwards to Find the Original Price

        Working backwards is useful when you only see the reduced price. Divide the final price by the share you paid: $324.45 ÷ 0.70 = $463.50. The same method checks whether a store's claimed original price is believable.

        $$\text{Original price} = \frac{\text{Final price}}{1 - \text{Discount \%}/100}$$

        Types of Discounts: Percentage Discount, Fixed Amount and More

        Most offers fall into a short list: a percentage off, a fixed amount off, a bundle, a volume tier or a payment-term reduction. The table below sums up how each one changes the price you pay.

        Discount typeHow it worksBest for
        Percentage offPrice × (1 − rate)Everyday shopping
        Fixed amountPrice − flat amountCoupons and vouchers
        StackedEach cut applies to the last priceSale plus coupon
        QuantityTiered rate by unitsBulk orders

        Retailers and suppliers use several structures, and each one calls for a slightly different calculation. Knowing which discount type you face tells you which formula to apply.

        Seasonal Discount and Clearance Sales

        A seasonal discount appears at predictable moments, such as end-of-year promotion periods, back-to-school weeks or holiday weekends. Clearance sales go further and cut the price deeply to empty shelves. A half price offer is the simplest case: 50% off means you pay exactly half of the original price.

        Quantity Discounts and Trade Discounts

        Quantity discounts reward larger orders, for example 5% off ten units and 12% off fifty units, so you compare the total cost of each tier. Trade discounts are different: a supplier offers them to distributors or resellers before the invoice is issued, and the end consumer never sees them. Bundles such as buy one get one free are promotional discounts, and you can convert them into a percentage decrease by dividing the free item's value by the total.

        Settlement Discount for Business Invoices

        A settlement discount is a small reduction for paying an invoice early, such as 2% off if paid within ten days. It belongs in your records separately from a trade discount, because it is only earned once the payment arrives, and it affects how VAT or GST is reported.

        Fake Discounts and How to Spot Them

        Fake discounts inflate the original price right before a sale so the markdown looks bigger than it is. Compare the claimed original price with earlier listings and with competing shops. If the reduced price matches what the item normally costs, the deal is not a deal, and you can negotiate or walk away.

        Discount Rate Calculator for Business Pricing: Margin, VAT and GST

        For a business, every discount has a cost. Enter a $50.00 unit price and a 20% cut into the tool and it returns a $40.00 final price and a $10.00 saving per unit; set that against your unit margin and your inventory levels and you can see how many extra sales the promotion must win to keep the same profit, because fixed costs stay put while each sale earns less.

        Invoices also need care. The tool's discount amount and final price can go straight onto the invoice as separate lines, and the VAT or GST is then charged on the discounted base, so the amount owed falls with the discount. A settlement discount may be adjusted after payment, so record the retail price, the discount and the net revenue as distinct entries and your supplier statements and bookkeeping will agree. The same discipline applies to e-commerce stores handling many orders a day.

        Discount Percentage Calculator Tips for Smarter Shopping

        A few habits make every offer easier to judge, whether you are comparing consumers' deals online or pricing stock for your own shop:

        • Compare offers by effective discount, not by the headline percent.
        • Apply sales tax last so your budget reflects what leaves your wallet.
        • Check that the original price is genuine before you trust a markdown.
        • Use the savings figure the tool reports to decide whether an offer is worth the purchase.
        • Remember that final price = original price × (1 − discount %/100); the tool applies it to every item you re-run.

        With these checks and the calculation shown above, you can read any price tag, coupon or stacked promotion with confidence.

        Discount Calculator questions

        How do I calculate a discount?

        Multiply the original price by the discount percentage divided by 100 to get the amount off, then subtract it from the original price. A quicker route is to multiply the original price by the share you still pay, for example 0.80 for 20% off.

        How do I find the discount percentage from two prices?

        Subtract the sale price from the original price, divide the result by the original price and multiply by 100. The discount calculator's effective discount figure shows the same result for any deal.

        Is a 20% discount followed by 10% the same as 30% off?

        No. Stacked discounts are multiplicative: the second percentage applies to the already reduced price, so 20% then 10% gives 28% off, not 30%. Use the double or triple discount option to see the exact effective discount.

        How can I find the original price from a discounted price?

        Divide the final price by the share you paid, which is 1 minus the discount rate. For instance, a $72 final price after 20% off means the original price was $72 ÷ 0.80 = $90.

        Is tax calculated before or after the discount?

        Most retail checkouts apply sales tax after the discount, so the discount also lowers the tax. Rules vary by location and product, so check your receipt; pick 'No, add sales tax' to add the tax rate to the discounted price.

        How do deals like 2 for 1 or 3 for 2 work?

        The cheapest item in the group is free, so you pay for the remaining items. The calculator shows what you pay, your total savings and the average cost per item.

        Is a 50% discount the same as half price?

        Yes. A 50% discount removes half of the original price, so the final price is exactly half of what the tag showed.

        What is the difference between a percentage discount and a fixed amount discount?

        A percentage discount takes a share of the original price, so it grows with the price. A fixed amount discount, such as a coupon for a set number of dollars, subtracts the same sum no matter the price.
