        How the Healthcare FSA Tax Savings Calculator Works

        An FSA lets you pay for qualified medical expenses with pretax money. Every dollar you contribute skips federal income tax, usually skips state and local taxes, and also skips payroll tax, so each dollar of an expense costs you less than a dollar. The healthcare FSA tax savings calculator multiplies your total planned contribution by your combined tax rate and shows the result as your tax savings potential for the year. Next, open the hsa goal calculator and enter your own details to see an estimate in seconds.

        \(\text{Annual tax savings} = \text{Contribution} \times (\text{Federal rate} + \text{State rate} + \text{Payroll tax rate})\)

        The payroll tax rate is the 7.65% Social Security and Medicare share. It is a fact of the tax code, so it does not change with your salary until you pass the Social Security wage base.

        Annual Salary and Tax Bracket Inputs

        Start with your annual salary, because income decides which tax bracket your contribution is shaved from. Add your filing status and state, since a single filer and a married couple filing jointly land in different brackets at the same paycheck size. A higher bracket means a bigger benefit from every pre-tax dollar.

        Medical Expenses and Dependent Care Expenses

        Next, list the medical expenses you already expect: doctor visits, co-pays, prescriptions, dental and vision care, chiropractic visits and over-the-counter items. If you also pay for a child or an adult relative, enter your dependent care expenses separately, such as day care, day camps, babysitters and elder care. The calculator totals each group, compares it with the maximum contribution, and tells you how much more you could add and still be fully reimbursed.

        Healthcare FSA vs Dependent Care FSA vs Limited Purpose FSA

        Employers often offer more than one account, and each pairs with its own limit. The IRS sets a $3,400 annual cap for a health care FSA in 2026, and your employer can choose a lower figure.

        • General purpose FSA (also called a medical FSA or HCFSA): covers qualified medical, dental and vision expenses.
        • Dependent care FSA: pays for day care, preschool, in-home caregivers and adult day programs while you work.
        • Limited purpose FSA: restricted to dental and vision expenses so it can sit beside a health savings account.

        Dependent Care FSA Limits

        A dependent care FSA covers a child under 13 or a spouse or relative who cannot care for themselves. The household limit was $5,000 for 2025 and rises to $7,500 from 2026. Enter your dependent care expenses and your spouse's details if you file jointly, and the calculator caps the contribution at that maximum.

        Limited Purpose FSA and HSA Pairing

        If you are covered by a high-deductible health plan, you may hold a health savings account (HSA). A general purpose account would block HSA contributions, but a limited purpose FSA does not. Unlike an FSA, HSA funds roll over every year and stay with you when you change jobs.

        Using the FSA Tax Savings Calculator for a Child's Braces

        Dana, a single parent earning $91,200, is staring at an orthodontist's treatment plan: $1,200 down and twelve payments of $120, which totals $2,640. Open enrollment closes Friday, so Dana opens the FSA tax savings calculator and builds the year from the paperwork on the desk.

        The entries go in one at a time: orthodontia $2,640, new glasses $318, prescriptions $544 and doctor visit co-pays $380. The calculator totals them at $3,882, then flags a problem: the IRS healthcare FSA limit for 2026 is $3,400, so the plan can fund only $3,400 of it. That leaves $482 that Dana will pay with after-tax money regardless.

        Dana then fills in the tax side: a 22% federal bracket, 5.25% state income tax and the 7.65% payroll rate, a combined 34.9%. The result card reads $1,186.60 in savings for the year, since $3,400 × 0.349 equals $1,186.60. With 24 semi-monthly paychecks, the deduction is $141.67 each, and the true cost of the $3,400 of care drops to $2,213.40.

        Two decisions follow from those numbers. Dana sets the election at the full $3,400 rather than $3,882, because anything above the limit is simply rejected by the plan. And because braces payments are reimbursed as they are made, Dana schedules the $482 glasses and co-pay balance as a card purchase in January, then reruns the calculator once with the glasses removed to confirm the savings do not change: they stay at $1,186.60, since the cap, not the expense list, now drives the answer.

        Eligible Expenses to Include in a Medical FSA

        Only eligible expenses count, so check the plan's list before you enter an amount. Typical qualifying expenses include:

        • Healthcare costs for an employee, spouse and children: deductibles, co-pays and prescriptions
        • Dental work and orthodontia, glasses, contact lenses and vision care
        • Dependent care expenses for day care, day camps and babysitters
        • Medical supplies and over-the-counter medicine such as pain relievers, bandages and allergy tablets
        • Any other account expenses your plan lists as eligible, which the tool adds to the total

        Premiums for your health insurance are usually not reimbursable, so leave them out of the calculation.

        Avoiding Forfeited Funds in a Flexible Spending Account

        Unlike a savings account, a flexible spending account follows a use-it-or-lose-it rule. Unused money at the end of the plan year can be forfeited. Some employers let you roll over a limited carryover amount instead, and others offer a grace period of about two and a half months. Ask your employer which one applies, because the calculator suggests a contribution based on your own expenses, not on what is left over. A dependent care FSA has no carryover, so every dollar needs claims by the plan deadline.

        Maximizing Tax Savings With a Healthcare FSA

        The savings figure this tool reports equals your contribution times your combined tax rate, so the savings only pay off when the amount you elect matches what you will actually spend. Enter last year's receipts plus anything planned, such as new glasses, a scheduled procedure or a prescription that changes dose, and elect that total in pre-tax dollars, up to the $3,400 limit.

        Estimate Expenses Conservatively in the Calculator

        It is easy to overestimate. Count only costs you are certain about, such as maintenance prescriptions and recurring dental cleanings, and treat a possible surgery as optional. A lower expense entry gives a lower tax savings figure but no forfeited balance. If your estimate lands between two round numbers, choose the lower one, then rerun the calculator at the next open season with a full year of data.

        Read the Deduction per Pay Period Result

        The calculator shows the contribution as a per-paycheck deduction next to the tax it removes. In the worked example above, a $103.92 deduction saves $35.59 in taxes each paycheck, so the net cost to take-home pay is only about $68.33 for $103.92 of spending power.

        When You Can Enroll in a Health Care FSA

        You can usually enroll only during your employer's open season, in the first weeks after you are hired, or after a qualified life event such as marriage or the birth of a child. Elections are fixed for the year, so run the FSA calculator before the deadline rather than after.

        Healthcare FSA Tax Savings Calculator questions

        How does the healthcare FSA tax savings calculator work?

        It adds up your expected eligible expenses, caps the total at the annual contribution limit, and multiplies the contribution by your combined federal and state tax bracket. The result is the tax you avoid by paying those costs with pre-tax dollars.

        How much can I contribute to a healthcare FSA?

        The IRS limit is $3,400 per year for a health care FSA, and your employer can set a lower amount. The dependent care limit is separate.

        What happens to unused FSA money?

        Under the use-it-or-lose-it rule, unused money at the end of the plan year can be forfeited. Some employers allow a limited carryover or a grace period instead, so check your plan before you choose a contribution.

        What is a limited expense FSA?

        A limited expense (limited purpose) FSA covers only dental and vision costs, which lets you pair it with a health savings account. Pick that option above and the calculator counts only dental, vision and orthodontia.

        Which expenses count as eligible?

        Typical eligible expenses include co-pays, deductibles, prescriptions, dental work, vision care, chiropractic visits and many over-the-counter items. Insurance premiums are generally not eligible, so check your plan's list.

        What tax bracket should I enter?

        Enter your combined federal and state marginal rate. If you are unsure, 30% is a common planning average; payroll taxes would add to your real savings.

        Can I change my FSA contribution during the year?

        Usually only during open season, shortly after you are hired, or after a qualified life event such as marriage or the birth of a child.

        Are the savings shown guaranteed?

        No. The figures are estimates for illustrative purposes and are not tax advice. Your actual savings depend on your income, filing status and plan rules.
