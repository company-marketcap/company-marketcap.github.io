        You are exempt if you receive VA compensation for a service-connected disability, are eligible for that compensation but receive retirement or active-duty pay instead, are a surviving spouse receiving Dependency and Indemnity Compensation, have a proposed or memorandum disability rating from a pre-discharge claim, or are on active duty and show evidence of a Purple Heart before closing.

        How the VA mortgage calculator estimates your monthly payment

        A VA home loan calculator starts with your purchase price, subtracts your down payment, adds the VA funding fee when it applies, and then spreads that loan amount over your chosen loan term at your interest rate. The result is your principal and interest. Property taxes, homeowners insurance and HOA fees are then added on top to give the estimated payment you would actually send every month. The down payment calculator is free to use with no sign-up, and works on desktop and mobile.

        Your lender calculates the principal and interest portion of a fixed rate loan with the standard amortization formula:

        $$M = P \times \frac{r(1+r)^{n}}{(1+r)^{n}-1}$$

        Here \(M\) is the monthly principal and interest, \(P\) is the total loan amount (including any financed funding fee), \(r\) is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12) and \(n\) is the number of monthly payments.

        What each input in the calculator means

        InputWhat it tells the calculator
        Home value or purchase priceThe price of the home before any down payment is subtracted.
        Down paymentCash you put toward the purchase. A VA loan needs none, but more down lowers the loan amount and the funding fee.
        Interest rateThe yearly rate on the loan. A fixed rate keeps principal and interest constant for the full term.
        Loan termThe repayment period, usually 15 or 30 years.
        VA eligibilityActive duty or veteran, reserves or National Guard, or surviving spouse.
        Used a VA loan beforeFirst-time use or subsequent use changes the funding fee percentage.
        Service-related disabilityA rating of 10% or more removes the funding fee entirely.
        Property taxesYearly real estate taxes, often estimated as a percentage of the home price.
        Homeowners insuranceYearly premium, typically a small percentage of the home value.
        HOA feesMonthly HOA dues, if the neighborhood or condo has an association.

        Property taxes, homeowners insurance and HOA dues

        The calculator adds three more inputs to principal and interest, because a lender expects you to budget for housing, not just the loan. Its default planning estimate is 1.2% of the home value per year for property taxes and 0.35% for homeowners insurance, though your county and insurer will set the real numbers. Hazard insurance and real estate taxes are usually collected into an escrow account, so the lender includes them in your monthly total.

        VA funding fee: what it adds to a VA purchase loan

        The VA funding fee is a one-time, government-backed program charge paid to the Department of Veterans Affairs to keep the VA mortgage loan program running. It replaces the private mortgage insurance that conventional lenders require, which is why a VA purchase loan has no monthly mortgage insurance. Because the program is government-backed, lenders can offer terms that build equity from day one. The funding fee depends on your down payment and on whether you have used the benefit before.

        Down paymentFirst-time useSubsequent use
        Less than 5%2.15%3.3%
        5% to less than 10%1.5%1.5%
        10% or more1.25%1.25%

        Other loan types carry their own percentages: an interest rate reduction refinance (IRRRL) is 0.5%, a loan assumption is 0.5%, and a manufactured home loan that is not permanently affixed is 1%. A cash-out refinance follows the same percentages as a purchase.

        Disability rating and funding fee exemptions

        If you have a service-related disability rating of 10% or higher, you pay no funding fee at all. Eligible surviving spouses are exempt as well. Guard and reserve members pay the same rates as active duty servicemembers and veterans. Because the fee is a percentage of the base loan, an exemption lowers both your loan balance and your monthly payment.

        Financed into the loan or paid upfront

        You can pay the funding fee in cash at closing (paid upfront) or have it financed into the loan. Financing keeps your cash in hand, but you pay interest on the fee for the life of the loan, so the monthly payment and the total of payments both rise.

        VA mortgage payment calculator example: a $342,000 home

        Here is how a VA loan calculator handles a real purchase. Suppose you are buying a $342,000 home with a 5% down payment of $17,100, a 6.375% fixed rate and a 30-year loan term. This is your first use of the benefit, and you have no disability rating. The base loan is $324,900. A 5% down payment lands in the 1.5% funding fee bracket, so the fee is $4,873.50. Financing it brings the total loan amount to $329,773.50.

        A VA loan lets you buy with no down payment, but the calculator shows why even a modest amount helps. A larger down payment cuts the base loan and also moves you into a lower funding fee bracket. Using the same $342,000 home, rate and term:

        15-year versus 30-year loan term

        A shorter loan term raises the monthly payment but slashes the interest. On the $329,773.50 loan, a 15-year term costs $2,850.07 a month and about $183,239 in total interest, against $2,057.36 and $410,875 over 30 years. A 20-year term sits in between at $2,434.49 a month.

        VA loan amortization schedule: where each payment goes

        The calculator's amortization schedule lists every payment for your inputs and splits it into interest and principal. In month one of the example, $1,751.92 goes to interest and only $305.44 reduces your balance. The mix shifts slowly: principal first overtakes interest in month 231, and the remaining balance is $278,687 after 10 years and $182,207 after 20 years. The calculator's chart and schedule let you see this for any rate or term.

        Extra payments and prepayment

        VA loans carry no prepayment penalty, so extra payments entered in the calculator's extra payment fields go straight to principal and shorten the schedule. A biweekly payment plan, a yearly lump sum or a few extra dollars each month all reduce total interest, and you can model each one by watching the total interest figure change.

        Checking a $286,900 home with a VA mortgage payment calculator

        A Marine veteran with a 30% service-connected disability rating is eyeing a $286,900 house and earns $6,150 a month before taxes. The first question is whether the payment fits under the 41% debt-to-income ceiling many VA lenders use as their benchmark.

        In the calculator, the home value goes in as $286,900, the down payment as $0, the interest rate as 6.125% and the loan term as 30 years. Property taxes are set at 1.1% of the price, which is $262.99 a month, and homeowners insurance at the $1,380 a year quoted by the agent, which is $115.00 a month. Under VA specifics, the disability box is ticked.

        The calculator returns $1,743.23 of principal and interest with a funding fee of $0. Adding taxes and insurance gives $2,121.23 a month, or 34.5% of gross income, comfortably inside the 41% benchmark.

        Next comes a second run with the disability box cleared, to see what the exemption is worth. A first-use, no-down-payment loan carries the 2.15% fee, which is $6,168.35. Financed into the loan, it lifts the balance to $293,068.35 and the principal and interest to $1,780.71, so the exemption saves $37.48 a month and about $7,324 in interest over 360 payments.

        The last run stress-tests the rate. At 6.625% the principal and interest becomes $1,837.05 and the all-in payment $2,215.04, or 36.0% of income. Still under 41%, so the veteran asks a lender for a pre-approval letter that cites the Certificate of Eligibility showing the funding fee exemption, and caps the offer at $286,900.

        Do you qualify for a VA mortgage loan?

        To use a VA home loan you need a valid Certificate of Eligibility (COE), and your lender will usually ask for your DD 214 discharge papers. In general you qualify through one of these routes:

        • 90 consecutive days of active duty service during wartime
        • 181 days of active service during peacetime
        • More than 6 years in the National Guard or reserves
        • Being the surviving spouse of a servicemember who died in the line of duty or from a service-related disability

        Veterans, active duty servicemembers and eligible spouses can reuse the benefit, and you do not need to be a first-time buyer. The home must be your primary residence.

        Closing costs on a VA purchase loan

        The funding fee is not the only cost at closing. Closing costs commonly include:

        • Loan origination fee, which covers the lender's processing costs
        • Discount points, paid to buy a lower rate; two points (2%) or less is considered reasonable
        • Credit report fee, which is not refunded if the loan never closes
        • Appraisal fee, because the VA sets the reasonable value of the property
        • Title insurance, to confirm there are no liens against the home
        • Recording fee, to record the deed
        • Hazard insurance and real estate taxes for the first year

        The estimated payment leaves these costs out, apart from a financed funding fee, so budget them separately as cash needed at closing. Sellers may pay some or all of these through seller concessions, up to 4% of the loan, but they are not obligated to.

        Pros and cons of a VA home loan

        Advantages over FHA and conventional loans

        Each of these shows up in the calculator's results as a lower monthly payment or a different loan balance.

        • No down payment, while a conventional loan normally asks for 5% and an FHA loan 3.5%; USDA is the only other common zero-down option
        • No monthly mortgage insurance, and typically lower interest rates and closing costs
        • VA appraisals help protect you from overpaying
        • A loan assumption lets a qualified buyer take over your loan later
        • Excellent refinance programs, including an IRRRL and a cash-out refinance

        Limits to keep in mind

        The first limit is the only one the calculator can price directly, through its funding fee input.

        • The funding fee can be expensive when you are not exempt
        • The loan cannot pay for planned home improvements, so a true fixer-upper is hard to finance
        • It covers owner-occupied homes only, not investment property or vacant land
        • Not every seller or lender has experience with VA paperwork, so choose a specialist

        Ways to lower your VA mortgage payment

        Once you see your number, a few levers change it quickly. Improve your credit score before applying, since the VA sets no minimum but lenders do. Shop several lenders for the lowest interest rate and compare APR, which folds in fees. Put more down if you can, extend the term if you need breathing room, and weigh an affordability check against your budget and your military housing allowance (BAH). If you already own, remember that a home sale can supply the down payment, and that the VA loan limit mostly matters when you have an existing VA loan.

        The figures here are for educational purposes only. Your lender's loan officer can confirm your rate, fees and approval. Run your own numbers through this VA mortgage loan calculator again whenever rates, prices or your down payment change.

        VA Mortgage Calculator questions

        How do I calculate a VA mortgage payment?

        Take the home price, subtract your down payment, add the VA funding fee if you are not exempt, and spread that loan amount over the loan term at your interest rate. Then add property taxes, homeowners insurance and any HOA fees to the principal and interest.

        What is the VA funding fee?

        It is a one-time fee paid to the Department of Veterans Affairs on most VA loans. For a purchase it is 2.15% on first use with less than 5% down, 1.5% with 5% to under 10% down and 1.25% with 10% or more down; subsequent use with less than 5% down is 3.3%.

        Who is exempt from the VA funding fee?

        Veterans with a service-related disability rating of 10% or more and eligible surviving spouses do not pay the fee. Select the disability option in the calculator to see the payment without it.

        Is a down payment required on a VA loan?

        No. Most eligible borrowers can buy with no down payment, but putting money down shrinks the loan amount and can drop you into a lower funding fee bracket.

        Should I finance the funding fee or pay it upfront?

        Financing keeps cash in your pocket at closing but adds the fee to the loan, so you pay interest on it. Paying it upfront lowers the monthly payment and the total interest.

        Does the monthly payment include closing costs?

        No. The estimate covers principal and interest, property taxes, insurance, HOA fees and any other yearly costs you enter. Closing costs such as origination fees, appraisal and title insurance are paid separately, unless a seller concession covers them.

        Do VA loans have private mortgage insurance?

        No. VA loans do not require monthly mortgage insurance, which is one reason the payment is often lower than a comparable conventional or FHA loan.

        Can I pay off a VA loan early?

        Yes. VA loans carry no prepayment penalty, so extra monthly, yearly or one-time payments reduce the balance and total interest. Enter them under extra payments to see the new payoff time.
